Overview

Sahil and I designed this arm almost completely from scratch: every arm part, linkage and assembly, the cycloidal actuators and the equations that define their curves, the motor driver circuitry and PCB, the forward kinematics tele-op controllers, and much of the code.

The things we couldn't do from scratch, like an inverse kinematics solver, we still studied and worked on. We either plan to do them in the future, or we have write-ups and tools we made to understand and use them. We've released all of it as open-source documentation at versorobotics.com.

Framework

What we actually made is a framework for building 6DOF arms. Our two arms are very different from each other, but they're built from the same parts and ideas.

My main contributions were the cycloidal function generator, designing the actuators, the mechanics for my arm, and the miniature tele-op controller.

Cycloidal actuators

We needed reducers to increase our NEMA stepper motors' torque output and precision. Rather than buy them, I generated them myself, partly to learn function driven modeling.

I made a parametric cycloidal gear generator with the Desmos API, set up so its equations can go straight into Fusion 360's Equation Driven Curve add-in. All of the actuators in the arm came from it (more on the math on the generator page).

The result is a 28:1 cycloidal gearbox with nearly zero backlash that's still backdrivable.

The actuator parts are laser cut, which is why everything in these photos is see-through. They can be 3D printed instead if laser cutting isn't an option.

Tele-op controller

The controller is a miniature version of the arm. You move its joints by hand and the arm copies them, which only needs forward kinematics since the joints match one to one.

Inverse kinematics (giving the arm a target and solving for the joint angles) is one of the things we didn't build from scratch, but it's something I want to come back to.

Build

We brought the price down to around $300, including fabrication and electronics. The arm is mostly 3D printed, with laser cut parts for the cycloidal actuators and covers.

It's by far the most time I've spent on any project. We each spent hundreds of hours on it across a full year of planning, designing, testing, failing and replanning.
