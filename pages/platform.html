Overview

I designed and built this motion platform for the RAM (Robotics, Automation and Mechatronics) Lab at Rutgers. It holds a treadmill and can tilt it in multiple axes, and it's now being used for locomotion research in the lab. The whole project took around one semester.

Background

I designed this to give researchers at the RAM lab a way to manipulate a treadmill in multiple axes without buying an expensive system. There are only a few commercially available ones, and they're 5–6 figures.

Professor Yi told me we had linear motors lying around in the lab that had gone unused for nearly a decade, so I designed the system around converting the linear motors' sliding into rotation of the platform.

Mechanism

After going through a few different joint configurations, I ended up with what's essentially an offset crank-slider: each linear motor moves a carriage, and a link from the carriage tilts the platform.

Build

After validating the design I did parts selection and CAD, then prototyping and iteration, and eventually assembly and testing.

In use
